# The perfect migration

*Illustrates: [01 Context](../principles/01-context.md) · [02 Quality × Quantity](../principles/02-quality-x-quantity.md)*

By every measure, the migration was a success. One and a half million files moved to the
new platform. Every KPI was green: 100% migrated, zero files lost, on time, on budget.

Then people started using it.

In the old system, nobody had ever written down where a document came from, what it
belonged to, or whether it was still the one that counted. They didn't need to. The
**folder path** said it. A file sitting under a client, a year and a project *was* that
client's, that year's, that project's. The structure held the provenance.

The migration moved the files. It didn't move the meaning of where they had been.

**We migrated every tree. And we lost the forest.**

## What we took from it
- A migration can be technically perfect and still lose information.
- Structure is information. Before you flatten it, write down what it meant.
- An AI reading those files today would understand every sentence, and still not know what
  each document *is*.
